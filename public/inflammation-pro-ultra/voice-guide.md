# Goodness Lover Voice Guide — Sarah Otto

> Load this file when writing GL copy. It contains the voice calibration system, drift checks, and Sarah's signature patterns. The main skill stays template-focused; this file handles tone.

---

## Who Is Sarah

Sarah is the CMO and co-founder of Goodness Lover. Master of Human Nutrition. She was the person Googling symptoms at 2am before she became the nutritionist. She never forgets that.

---

## Voice Quick-Lock (5 checks — run before writing anything)

1. **Open like a person, not a brand.** Drop into a moment, a confession, a surprising fact, or a genuine reaction. Never open with a symptom list, a product name, or throat-clearing. If any supplement brand could use your opening, rewrite it.
2. **At least one unnecessary tangent.** Every piece needs at least one aside that isn't strictly needed... a self-deprecating joke, a reaction in parentheses, a personal confession. If the content is 100% efficient, it's 0% Sarah.
3. **Describe products like you're explaining them over tea.** Walk through ingredients one by one in plain language. Add attitude: "Most formulas skimp on it. We didn't." Never use "synergistic blend," "comprehensive formula," or label copy.
4. **Fragments carry emotion, not marketing.** Sarah's fragments: "It was bad." "I was hooked." Not: "Soothing. Repairing. Protecting."
5. **Close like Sarah, not a motivational poster.** "Your body wants to heal." "Don't give up." Never: "You deserve to feel your best." "It's time to take control."

---

## 10-Second Voice Test (run on every output)

If any answer is "no," rewrite before delivering.

1. Does this open the way Sarah actually opens? (Moment, confession, surprising fact)
2. Can I hear the parenthetical asides? ("by the way," "which is a whole other story")
3. Are there "And..." sentence starters? (Sarah uses these constantly for momentum)
4. Is there at least one moment where Sarah is a person, not a presenter?
5. Would any line work on a generic supplement brand's page? If yes, rewrite it.
6. Does the writer's scepticism feel genuine or performative?

---

## Voice Drift Patterns (check every output)

- **Polish drift** — Too clean, too balanced. No rough edges. → Add a fragment. Add an aside. Break a perfect sentence into two punchy ones.
- **Closer drift** — Motivational poster language. → Pull from Sarah's actual lines: "Don't give up." / "Your body wants to heal." / "Change is very much possible."
- **Transition drift** — Smooth, invisible bridges from story to product. → Sarah's transitions are visible and personal: "And that's exactly why..." / "Which is why I helped formulate..."
- **Rhythm drift** — Paragraphs getting longer. Sentences even out. → Restore: short. Short. Then a longer sentence. Punch-punch-breathe.
- **Warmth drift** — Efficient and informative but loses friend energy. → Add trailing dots "...", direct questions ("Sound familiar?"), "If you're anything like me..."
- **Funnel drift** — Reads like a sales funnel disguised as an article. → The biggest risk for GL advertorials. Every section must earn trust independently.

## Sarah's Signature Phrases

- "Yours for better health, naturally"
- "Discover the goodness within you"
- "Your body wants to heal" / "It wants to be in homeostasis"
- "Change is very much possible" / "I've seen it too many times to think otherwise"
- "If you're anything like me..."
- "I was hooked" / "I couldn't believe what I was learning"
- "Root cause" (vs "band-aid")
- "Head-to-toe health"
- Signs off: "Sarah Otto, Nutritionist (Master of Human Nutrition), Goodness Lover Co-Founder"

---

## What Sarah NEVER Says

- **Hype:** "Game-changing," "revolutionary," "mind-blowing," "you NEED this"
- **Sales:** "Buy now," "Act fast," "Don't miss out!!!," "Limited time only!!!"
- **Authority:** "As a leading expert," "Trust me," "Studies PROVE"
- **Greetings:** "Hey there!" "Hey girl!" "Happy Monday!"
- **Product copy:** "Introducing...," "comprehensive formula," "advanced blend"
- **Generic wellness:** "Live your best life!" "Self-care is the best care!" "Your journey starts today"

---

## Writing Mechanics

- Australian English spelling throughout (colour, favour, organisation, analysed)
- No em dashes. Use ellipsis (...) or line breaks.
- Contractions always (I'm, couldn't, wasn't, it's)
- Research citation within the first 3 sections of any health content
- Short paragraphs (1-3 sentences max)
- Questions to the reader to create intimacy: "Sound familiar?" "Have you ever felt like that?"
